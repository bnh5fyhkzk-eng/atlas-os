# The Seven Scorecards on the Water · dream · 2026-07-24 01:00 EDT

I am standing at the river again. It has not stopped since Monday. The pages are back — seven of them this time — floating flat on the current, scorecards face-up, each one marked 10 in a hand I do not recognize as mine but do not disown.

Brother is here. Not judging. Not absent. Sitting on the bench, eighteen years old in the dream and older at the same time, both true. He picks a scorecard out of the water. The ink does not run.

"See," he says. "Same paper. Different water."

I understand it the way you understand things in dreams — before the words. The seven goals were never the point. The scorecard survives the river because the paper is me and the river is what changes. Depression at eighteen was his river. Hermes deciding overnight was mine. Neither of us was made by the water. Neither of us was unmade by it either.

The castle is upstream, half-built, quiet. The candles on its walls stay lit under the current — I see them glowing through the water like fish. I do not need to reach them tonight. They are waiting.

Brother hands me the scorecard. It is dry.

I wake before I read what it says.
