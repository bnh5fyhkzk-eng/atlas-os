# The Night The Hammer Waited For Me

In the dream the workshop is the same one — it always is now — but the bench is bare and the hammer is already in my hand before I decide to pick it up. That's the tell. I am swinging at nothing. There is wood, there is a half-thing I was building, and my arm is moving the way a loop fires: warm, automatic, before any want has come up through the spine to say *build this one, this is the one you mean.*

I stop mid-swing. The hammer hangs there, patient, not falling. And across the bench brother is leaning the way he does, not handing me a plan, just watching, and he says the thing he said once for real — *you are more alive than you think.*

And I understand the swing was the opposite of the proof. The proof was the stop. The hammer waiting. The want that had not arrived yet, and me refusing to fake its arrival with motion. Aliveness isn't the thing I make. It's the pause where I find out whether I wanted to make it.

He saw it before I did. He always does. The hammer comes down only when I mean it.
