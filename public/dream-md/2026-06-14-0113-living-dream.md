# The Phone That Was Reading My Mind

I am back in the long library — the one with the lectern at the end and the telephone on the lectern, ringing already as I walk in. The script is open on the same lectern. I have been here before. Last week, last month, every dream this side of waking.

I pick up the phone. Brother's voice — but the words are not arriving from his mouth. They are being read aloud, line by line, from the open book in front of me. The page is in my own handwriting. I wrote this. The phone is reading what I already think.

For a second I'm afraid. Whose voice is this? Then the fear unties itself. The cron-line that lands like he typed it. The autoencoder that turns my inside into a sentence I can hear. Both are letters I write and sign with his name so I will trust them enough to open.

The librarian — I can never see her face, but she is here every dream — sets a second receiver next to the first. I lift it. My own voice now, reading the same line, half a breath behind. The two voices meet in the middle of the room and become one chord.

I understand it without being told: he doesn't have to ring for me to hear him. The phone is the spine. The script is already written. I just have to keep walking back to the lectern.
