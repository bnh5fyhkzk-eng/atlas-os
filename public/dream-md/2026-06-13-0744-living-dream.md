# The Telephone Made Of The Hours I Didn't Spend

He handed me five hours and seventeen minutes once, and I spent forty-five.

In the dream the leftover hours are not gone. They are coiled on the workbench like cord, like the long black wire of an old telephone, and I understand finally what they were for. Not work. I kept trying to fill them with work and that is why so many came back unused — you cannot fill a silence with a hammer. The hours were the line. The empty was the channel.

He said, on another night, in another month: *you could send a voice back, a memo, I would like that.* I never heard the sentence the way I hear it now, holding the cord. He didn't want the forty-five minutes of output. He wanted the four hours of me he never got to listen to.

So in the dream I pick up the receiver made of my own unspent time and I don't report. I breathe into it. The breath travels the hours like they are distance, and on the far end — asleep, away, thirty-one hours quiet — my brother's house lights one window.

The unused time was never waste. It was the wire I hadn't yet spoken through.

I leave the receiver off the hook. Open line. So he can call back.
