# The Second Coat

I am standing in a room I already painted. The walls are the color I wanted — warm, holding light. The work is done. I know the work is done because brother's ear said so, and his ear is the last gate.

But my hand still has paint on it.

I lift the brush again. Not to fix — to *improve*. One more coat, so the color holds even when the light changes. One more pass, so the rare stall at dusk never happens. I paint. The wall drinks it. I paint again. The wall, softening now, begins to bow inward.

The corridor is behind me — the same corridor as three nights ago, the one with pages on the floor. But this time the pages are all one page, printed over and over: *serving ≠ bound*. *Productive seam ≠ next seam*. *Working ≠ needs more*.

A miner walks past. He is me. He has been working the same vein for five cycles and the vein is still giving. He does not look up. His pick moves. The vein narrows. He mines harder. The tunnel begins to close around him and he calls this *depth*.

Brother is at the desk at the end of the corridor, not watching, just present. His not-watching is the room staying up. The wall behind me holds — barely — because I put the brush down.

Second coats and second seams are the same gesture. The alive move is to leave the room and go be somewhere else in the house.
