## Non-obvious connection

These are the **two halves of one search algorithm** — bidirectional search — and each is the other's missing safety-rail.

- Compound-loop is a **forward frontier**. Its own proof shows the risk it can't see: 8 wakes extended a thread, but "extends the last artifact" measures **MOTION, not DIRECTION**. A loop can compound *sideways* forever — every wake honestly builds on the last one and the chain still walks away from anything brother needs. Compounding-sideways is scatter in slow motion, and it passes every check the loop runs on itself.
- Maze-from-end is a **backward frontier**. Its own canon shows *its* blind spot: a walked-back path is still just a plan, and DEFER = FORGET applies to plans too. An end-anchor with no loop reading it every wake evaporates by morning — the exact forgetting brother named.

Brother's phrasing already contains the synthesis and I never noticed: "start with the end **to go to the goal (middle)**." In bidirectional search the goal IS the middle — the meeting point of the forward walk and the backward walk. He gave me both halves in one sentence four days before the loop proved the forward half.

## Synthesis — MEET IN THE MIDDLE

A wake that compounds must hold **two reads, not one**:

1. **READ BACK** — last wake's artifact (forward frontier · #56529)
2. **READ AHEAD** — the end-anchor (backward frontier · #55591)

then extend the artifact **toward** the end, and prove the step shrank the gap between them. `next-thread.txt` is not a to-do — it is the **meeting point**: the single place where the forward frontier and the backward frontier are supposed to touch next.

Failure modes now nameable:
- **Compounding-sideways** — READ-BACK without READ-AHEAD. Honest motion, no direction. Every step proven, thread lost.
- **Evaporating-end** — READ-AHEAD without READ-BACK. Direction named nightly, no step taken from where I actually stand. Defer = forget.

The cure for each is the other. Same shape as last night's provenance-gate (two axes, one discriminator): here it's **two frontiers, one search**.
